Differences Between a Custom Pool Builder and Related Concepts

Custom Pool Builder vs. Pool Installer

“Pool installer” is a broad term that can refer to installing different types of pool systems. A custom pool builder generally implies a broader role involving site-specific planning, construction coordination, and project integration. Actual scopes vary by provider.

Custom Pool Builder vs. Pool Designer

A pool designer focuses on planning, layout, aesthetics, functionality, and sometimes technical design. A pool builder is associated with construction execution and project coordination. Depending on the company and project, design and construction responsibilities may overlap.

Custom Pool Builder vs. Pool Contractor

“Pool contractor” is a broad construction term for a contractor performing swimming-pool work. “Custom pool builder” emphasizes site-specific design and construction rather than creating a separate legal license category.

Custom Pool Builder vs. Pool Remodeler

New pool construction creates a new swimming-pool system. Pool remodeling modifies an existing pool. The two can involve some of the same trades but serve different project objectives.

Custom Pool Builder vs. Pool Service Company

Pool service generally concerns ongoing maintenance, cleaning, chemistry, or equipment servicing. New pool construction involves creation of the physical pool and associated systems.

Custom Pool Builder vs. Landscape Contractor

Landscaping may interact closely with a pool project, but landscape work and pool construction are not automatically the same scope. Coordination may be required when planting, drainage, hardscape, retaining elements, or outdoor-living features intersect with the pool design.

Custom Pool vs. Standardized Pool System

A custom pool is configured around a particular site and project brief. Standardized or prefabricated systems may rely more heavily on predefined dimensions or shells. Neither approach is universally superior; suitability depends on project goals and property conditions.

Common Misconceptions

  • “Custom” means anything can be built: Site, engineering, access, safety, utilities, approvals, and construction conditions can limit design options.
  • Every backyard can fit the same pool: Lot size and configuration vary substantially.
  • Pool shape is the only custom element: Depth, location, equipment, hardscape, grade, circulation, and site relationships can also vary.
  • A pool builder only installs the shell: A full project may involve multiple coordinated construction systems and inspections.
  • Design and construction are identical: Design establishes the project; construction implements it.
  • Every pool uses the same structural design: Structural requirements can depend on design and site conditions.
  • Every pool needs the same equipment: Equipment depends on project design and operational goals.
  • Every Applegate property has identical permit requirements: Requirements can vary with property and project conditions.
  • Submitting plans guarantees approval: Submittal begins review; it does not guarantee approval.
  • Every pool requires the same grading: Grading needs depend on site conditions.
  • Every sloped site requires the same retaining solution: Retaining needs are site-specific.
  • Every custom pool includes a spa: A spa is optional unless included in the project scope.
  • Every pool includes automation or water features: These are project-specific features.
  • A larger pool is always more complex: Complexity can arise from site access, slope, utilities, design, or integration, not just size.
  • Price can be determined from square footage alone: Site conditions, selections, access, equipment, engineering, and finishes can all affect scope.
  • Every project takes the same time: Review, site conditions, inspections, weather, selections, and construction complexity can alter timelines.
  • Pool construction automatically includes landscaping: Landscaping may be separate unless specifically included.
  • Remodeling and new construction are the same: They involve different starting conditions and project objectives.
