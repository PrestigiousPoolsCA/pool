3. Risk Categories Associated With Misuse

Incorrect marketing practices can create several categories of operational and compliance risk.

  • Expectation risk: Overstated claims about pricing, timelines, features, or outcomes may cause customers to expect results that are not realistic for their specific property.
  • Construction risk: Poorly communicated design assumptions may overlook important considerations such as drainage, grading, access, equipment placement, or material suitability.
  • Contract risk: Marketing statements that do not align with written agreements may create confusion regarding project scope, included features, or responsibilities.
  • Local trust risk: Incorrect geographic claims, fabricated project examples, or misleading service-area statements can weaken credibility with homeowners and search systems.
  • Platform enforcement risk: Misleading advertisements, inaccurate business information, manipulated reviews, or unsupported claims may result in reduced visibility or account restrictions.
  • Reputation risk: A mismatch between marketing promises and actual project delivery can result in complaints, negative reviews, and reduced referral confidence.

These risks are increased in custom construction because each property has unique characteristics. A statement that may be accurate for one project may not apply universally to every Drytown property.
