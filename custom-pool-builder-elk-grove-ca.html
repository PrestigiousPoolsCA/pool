4. Differences Between Custom Pool Building and Related Concepts

Custom Pool Construction vs. Standardized Pool Installation

The primary distinction between custom pool construction and a standardized installation is the degree to which the project is developed around the specific property and owner requirements. A standardized product generally begins with predetermined dimensions, configurations, or options. A custom project begins with site and use requirements and develops the design from those inputs.

Custom Pool Building vs. Pool Remodeling

Pool remodeling generally concerns an existing pool and may involve finishes, equipment, features, decking, or other modifications. Custom pool building, as used in this standard, primarily refers to planning and constructing a new residential pool. Remodeling and new construction can involve similar materials or equipment but represent different project scopes.

Pool Design vs. Pool Construction

Pool design establishes the planned configuration, dimensions, features, spatial relationships, and related specifications. Pool construction is the physical implementation of the approved project. The two functions are related but should not be treated as interchangeable: a design decision can affect excavation, structure, equipment, materials, sequencing, and cost.

Pool Construction vs. Outdoor-Living Design

Outdoor-living design may encompass patios, landscaping, kitchens, shade structures, seating, lighting, and other exterior features. A custom pool can form a central part of that environment without making every outdoor improvement part of the pool construction scope. The project documents should clarify which elements are included.

5. Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: “Custom” only means choosing a unique pool shape. Customization can include placement, dimensions, depths, access, equipment, finishes, features, circulation, and integration with the surrounding property.
  • Misconception: A larger pool is automatically a better pool. Appropriate size depends on intended use, available space, circulation, maintenance expectations, features, and the amount of outdoor area the owner wants to preserve.
  • Misconception: The lowest initial proposal identifies the lowest-cost project. Proposals may contain different scopes, materials, equipment, allowances, exclusions, and features. Meaningful comparison requires review of what is actually included.
  • Misconception: A visual design alone determines construction feasibility. Site conditions, access, project requirements, technical evaluation, and other property-specific factors may affect implementation.
  • Misconception: Every Elk Grove pool follows an identical approval process. Applicable requirements can depend on jurisdiction, property conditions, project characteristics, and scope and should be independently confirmed for the specific project.
