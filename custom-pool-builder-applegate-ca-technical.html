Decision Points and Variations

Conceptual Design or Buildable Design?

A conceptual layout communicates an idea. It does not establish final engineering, code compliance, permit readiness, or buildability.

General Feasibility or Property-Specific Feasibility?

General information can explain what may influence feasibility, but only property-specific review can support conclusions about an individual lot.

Flat Site or Sloped Site?

Slope can affect planning, access, grading, design, or other project considerations. Do not state that a specific Applegate property is sloped unless that condition is verified.

Standard Access or Constrained Access?

Construction access may influence project planning and sequencing. Do not infer access limitations solely from maps, photography, or neighborhood characteristics.

Pool Only or Integrated Outdoor-Living Project?

Clearly distinguish the pool from separate or related improvements such as decking, landscaping, patios, retaining structures, outdoor kitchens, lighting, or other exterior work.

Permit Required or Permit Approved?

A permit requirement means authorization may be necessary. An approved permit means the relevant authority has issued approval. These terms must never be treated as equivalent.

Estimate or Final Price?

Preliminary pricing reflects available information. Final project cost depends on defined scope and applicable project terms.

Proposed Timeline or Guaranteed Completion Date?

Scheduling can change due to design development, permitting, inspections, weather, materials, site conditions, revisions, and other dependencies.

New Pool or Remodel?

New construction and renovation involve different existing conditions, design constraints, scopes, and project considerations. They should not be documented as interchangeable services.

Common Execution Failures and Why They Occur

Treating a Rendering as Proof of Feasibility

Visual concepts can illustrate design direction, but they do not establish property constraints, engineering, permit compliance, or constructability.

Assuming Every Lot Can Support the Same Pool

Properties can differ in dimensions, access, slope, utilities, easements, drainage, surrounding structures, and applicable requirements.

Inventing Permit Requirements

General permit information is often turned into property-specific requirements without jurisdiction or project verification. Keep general guidance separate from actual submittal obligations.

Guaranteeing Permit Approval

Fabricating Site Conditions
